Capstone. For fun.

For eight weeks you've learned to use AI to build personal tools. This week is the celebration.

To be clear up front: nothing built this week is going into a real ATN roadmap. No one is launching the winning pitch. There's no real ad spend, no PRD, no follow-up meeting. The winning team wins bragging rights. That's the whole prize.

The premise — "prototype a product ATN should build and advertise on native and Meta" — is a setup, not a brief. It's the playground we're using to celebrate everything you learned. ATN is best-in-class at customer acquisition; that's our world, so it's the world we're pitching in. But it's a pitch competition, not a product strategy meeting.

So pitch hard. Build something fun. Treat it like the stakes are real even though they aren't — the point is to use the whole stack you've built up over eight weeks, in a team, against a deadline, in front of execs who are graciously playing along as judges.

The prompt
Pitch a product that should be built and advertised on Native and Meta.

Your team has 3 hours across Monday and Tuesday to build a pitch-ready prototype of a product you'd want to see ATN launch. The pitch needs to be backed by a believable user flow: ad → landing page → product in action → "pay to unlock" CTA. Built in Claude Code, demoed locally Wednesday.

You're pitching the business as if it were real. Any B2C category. The product itself doesn't need to use AI — but you're building it in Claude Code in 3 hours, so the build itself will.

Aim high. Make the judges wish ATN were going to build it.

The pitch

One deliverable. A 5-minute pitch + 2-minute Q&A delivered live Wednesday. The pitch has three parts, in this order:

#PartFormat
1 WHAT — What is the product? Open with this. One sentence a stranger could understand. 1 slide
2 WHY — Why this product? What framework led you here? An unmet need you spotted, a category ATN's media-buying edge unlocks, a competitor doing something badly, a trend you're betting on. Judges weight this equally with the demo — decision quality matters as much as execution. 1 slide
3 DEMO — Show the funnel end-to-end. Required: at least 2 ad concepts, the landing page, and the product in action. The product can be a real clickable prototype (built in Claude Code, demoed locally on Wednesday) or generated images of the user flow — whichever you can produce credibly in 3 hours. Inline in pitch
Don't waste time on these

Login, accounts, payments processing, mobile apps, anything that needs a database. If you find yourself building auth, scope down.

Don't deploy. Demo locally Wednesday — screenshare your laptop. One less thing to figure out.

Don't over-polish the slides. The WHAT + WHY is 1–2 slides. The demo is the rest. If you're picking fonts, you're losing time.

Teams

6 teams of 4–5 people each, pre-assigned. Every team gets one executive plus a mix of disciplines — engineering, marketing, product, creative. Cross-team experience matters more than discipline label: you'll likely be working with people you haven't built with before. That's the point.

Team rosters will be announced in #ai-unlock10x on Friday 5/16. Each team gets a dedicated Slack channel.

Judging

The six executives are the judging panel. Each exec ranks the five teams they can judge (cannot vote for their own team) across three dimensions — for each dimension, 1st = 5 pts down to 5th = 1 pt.

A team's total = sum of points across all 3 dimensions, across all 5 judges. Highest total wins overall. Per-dimension winners called out too. Tiebreaker: Luke's vote.

The three dimensions:

Dimension 1
Most believable on a CAC basis
Could ATN actually acquire customers profitably here? Would you put real Meta/native spend behind it? Honest CPA economics?
Dimension 2
Best pitch
Regardless of concept strength — whose materials, narrative, and demo were most polished? Who told the story best?
Dimension 3
Most built out
Depth of execution. Real working prototype, substantive demo, ads + landing that look like real product? Or thin?
Why this is the capstone

Eight weeks ago, most of the cohort had never built anything in Claude Code, never written a CLAUDE.md, never thought of a chat tool as a thing that could ship a product. Now you're being asked to use all of it — teamed up with people you haven't worked with closely, in 3 hours total, in front of an audience — to deliver a real-feeling business pitch.

The "no roadmap follow-up" framing is a permission slip. You don't have to defend whether this is the right move for ATN, or whether the unit economics actually pencil, or whether the team will be staffed against it. You get to play. Pitch the most fun idea your team can credibly demo. Make it land. Then go home Wednesday and tell people what your team built.

The winning team gets bragging rights. The whole cohort gets to see what their classmates can do when the constraint is "make something real-feeling in 3 hours." That's the capstone.
